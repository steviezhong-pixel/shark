Interpreter vs sourcing agent: which does your trip actually need?

Where an interpreter is enough, where you need supplier development work, and what either role will not do.

"I need an interpreter" and "I need a sourcing agent" are different purchases that overlap on the surface. Buying the wrong one wastes both money and your trip days.

A business interpreter is the right tool when

  • You know which suppliers you want to meet and how to evaluate them; you mainly need accurate two-way communication.
  • You make the decisions and just remove the language friction.
  • Your technical questions are answerable by the supplier during the meeting.

A sourcing / supplier-development role earns its cost when

  • No supplier list yet exists, or the one from search engines is unfiltered.
  • You need pre-trip work: capability screening, MOQ/ customization checks, appointment setting with the right contact level.
  • Quotes, samples and revisions need chasing after you fly home.

What neither role should claim

  • Audits or certification verification — that is accredited third-party work; we coordinate inspectors, we do not certify.
  • Guarantees about order results. Meetings and records can be delivered; orders cannot be promised by anyone honest.

A middle screen for honest providers

Ask any provider two questions: "who receives the supplier's payment — me or you?" and "how do you record what I agreed in each meeting?" A pure interpreter should say "you pay them directly"; a coordination service should provide written records of requirements, decisions and open items. If answers to both are vague, treat the rest of their promises the same way.
